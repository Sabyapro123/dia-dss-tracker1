    How the system works

    PartWhat happens
    QR label on every binEach bin carries a label with the item code, the depot and the lot. The same item has the same code at every depot, so the system knows that pumps at East Depot are the pumps North Depot needs.
    Scan at every movementA storekeeper scans the label whenever stock is received or issued. The stock figure on screen therefore always matches the shelf.
    Three levels per itemReorder point equals monthly use × lead time + reserve. Maximum level equals reorder point + three months of use. The reserve is never offered for transfer.
    Transfer before buyingWhen a depot falls below its reorder point, the system looks for stock above the maximum level at the other two depots. It proposes a transfer when that is cheaper and faster than a purchase, and a single combined order for whatever is left.
    Consignment trackingAn approved transfer gets its own consignment QR. The sending depot scans it out, its progress shows on the map, and the receiving depot scans it in after inspection. In real use the position would come from a GPS tracker on the vehicle.
    Review, not disposalStock that will expire unused, or that exceeds what its equipment will ever need, goes to a review board for retain, redistribute, repair, return or dispose decisions.
    Audit logEvery action is recorded with the role, the time and the reason. Auditors can test, for example, whether anything was bought while another depot held excess.

    In a real deployment the database sits on a secure government network, each user signs in with a role, and phones or handheld scanners at each depot connect to it. This demo keeps everything inside the page, and Reset demo restores the starting figures.
